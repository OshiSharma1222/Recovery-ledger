The problem

ScreenHomepage, still. Let the count-up finish before you start talking.

A subscription payment fails. The customer got the service, the business earned the money, and the money never arrived.

In India this happens constantly, because UPI Autopay and e-mandates fail in ways cards do not. Mandates expire. Amount caps get breached. Customers revoke permission silently.

And almost every billing system responds the same way: retry on day one, day three, day five. Without ever asking why it failed.

0:28

The insight

ScreenScroll slowly to the live ledger preview, then to the four steps.

But the reason matters enormously.

No balance? That is a timing problem. Retry just after payday and you will probably get paid.

Expired mandate? A retry can never work. The permission to charge is gone, so the bank refuses every single attempt, forever.

In my simulation, twenty-six percent of at-risk money is unrecoverable by construction. A fixed schedule burns three attempts on every one of those rows, every cycle, and gets nothing back.

Lane 1, a real row

ScreenClick Ledger. Pause on the table. Then open a mandate_revoked row.

So I built one ledger. Every rupee the merchant is owed and does not have, in one table.

Here is a real row. The bank sent back payment_cancelled. That string is all a merchant actually gets.

My classifier joins it with the merchant's own mandate record, the cap and the expiry date they already hold, and resolves it: the customer revoked the mandate.

So the engine does this. It abandons the row. Twenty-eight thousand rupees, and it deliberately spends nothing.

That is the whole thesis. Recovery is resource allocation, and the hard part is knowing what not to chase.
